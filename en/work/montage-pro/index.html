Montage Pro, which began as AudioSync Pro, reads a shoot day with one folder per camera or recorder, matches every clip by its audio, stacks each recording session across the cameras and writes a Final Cut Pro 7 XML for Premiere Pro, plus a JSON sync report.

The editor’s problem

Camera clocks cannot be trusted.

A multicamera talk-show day arrives as separate files per camera, with split takes and stopped recordings. The engine places clips by the sound they recorded, keeps each camera on its own track, and flags anything it cannot place confidently instead of dropping it.

How the sync works

Decode. Vote. Refine. Assemble.

Original media is never modified or transcoded; only audio is decoded, in memory.

01

Decode audio only

Each clip’s first audio stream becomes 8 kHz mono, high-passed and normalised.

02

Vote on the offset

60-second envelope chunks cross-correlate and vote; the winning cluster gives offset and clock drift.

03

Refine below a millisecond

20-second waveform windows use GCC-PHAT-family correlation with sub-sample refinement.

04

Assemble and cross-check

Matches become sync groups; one that disagrees with the timeline by more than 500 ms is marked inconsistent.

Known-offset tests

The failing version was kept as evidence.

On a realistic synthetic day — three cameras, three sessions, split files and per-camera drift — v0.1.0 placed 4 of 12 clips and failed; v0.2.0 placed 12 of 12 within 0.5 ms. The XML is also parsed independently by OpenTimelineIO’s fcp_xml adapter.

Verification boundary

Private and in development on a v0.4.0 branch. Premiere Pro’s import of the current XML and the planned in-Premiere panel remain unverified until tested on an editor’s Mac, and the macOS packages were built without a runtime check. Placement uses audio only — embedded timecode is not used — and clock drift is measured and reported, not corrected. Accuracy figures come from synthetic days with known offsets. AI-assisted build; Mohamed specified, reviewed and verified it.

# THE ALBUM — director's cut notes for the photoreal pass

Not published with the page. This is the reasoning behind pass 6: which of the
25 Wan clips lands where, why, and what the scroll hand is doing while it does.

The film already had a spine — six acts at 84 BPM, Prologue Silence → Prologue
Contact → Map → Build → Gate → Prove → Finale — and captions that work. None of
that moves. What changes is that the film stops being *drawn* and starts being
*photographed*, and then, after the needle lifts, it stops being a film about a
room and becomes a film about what happens inside the listener.

So: **Side A is the room. Side B is the record.**

---

## The shape

A record has a shape and this cut borrows it exactly.

```
SIDE A   ident → six acts, each opening on a photoreal room → needle up
                 ── the audience has been sitting still for six acts ──
FLIP     one beat of black. "Turn it over."
SIDE B   01 portal ─ 02 tunnel ─ 03 corridor ─ 04 city      ← the build
         05 parallax ─ 06 vinyl ─ 07 highway                ← THE DROP
         08 the eye ─ 09 deep space ─ 10 the pull-back      ← the breakdown
         11 the return ─ 12 one line                        ← the outro
FINALE   the film's own ending, unchanged, now earned twice
```

The drop is at leg 07 and it is not a colour change or a flash — it is the
first time in eleven minutes of scrolling that something has **speed**. Legs 01
to 06 are all pushes: doors, tunnels, corridors, a dive. Nothing is travelling;
the camera is. At leg 06 the groove of a record becomes a road, and at leg 07
there is a car on it doing an implied 200. That contrast is the drop. It costs
nothing and it works because the six acts before it were patient.

---

## Side A — the room, six times

The room set is seven takes inside one immutable room. Six of them are the six
acts made real; the seventh is a duplicate. Each act now opens on its
photoreal hero, then hands to the Cycles plate that shows the same claim as
solved geometry, then to the code scene that draws it live. Photograph → render
→ code, three times per act. That order is the argument: the pretty thing first,
then the proof it was built, then the proof it can be *drawn*.

| Act | Clip | The move | Why this take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue · Silence | `room01-silence` | Opens near-black, the room resolving out of nothing, ending on the mark and the deck | The only take whose first frame is genuinely dark. "Silence has a line" needs a frame with no line in it yet. |
| Prologue · Contact | `room02-contact` | Wide room → dolly down onto the deck → extreme macro of the platter, one green streak across the vinyl | The furthest-travelling take in the set. It ends where the caption ends: on the record. Contact lands with the scroll. |
| Act I · Map | `room03-runway` | A green light column climbs from the table mark to the wall mark while the camera tracks past the console | Act I is "find the groove". This is the only take where light *moves through* the room on a beat instead of the camera moving through light. |
| Act II · Build | `room04-build` | A lateral track down the desk — monitor, keyboard, the deck behind it — ending on the workstation | Build is the working act. It is the only take that treats the desk as the subject rather than as furniture at the edge of a wide. |
| Act III · Gate | `room05-lounge` | A slow, held track along the lounge wall: headphones neon, the posters, the neon script, the guitar | Gate is suspense by subtraction. The camera has to be patient and pass things without stopping. This take never arrives anywhere, which is the point. |
| Act IV · Prove | `room06-chorus` | Opens out from the desk to the whole room — TV wall, deck, sofa, everything at once | Prove is the return of everything. The frame has to get *bigger*, and this is the take that opens rather than pushes. |

**Unused room take:** the seventh clip covers the same room as `room06-chorus`
with about a third of the camera travel — the flatter alternate of the same
setup. Two takes of one room, and the chorus wants the one that moves.

---

## The flip

One short scene between the Finale and Side B, black, one line of type, no
footage. It exists for a physical reason: after eleven acts of continuous
imagery the audience needs a beat with nothing in it or the second movement
reads as more of the first. On a record you stand up and turn it over, and that
takes about four seconds. This is those four seconds.

---

## Side B — the flight

Twelve clips, sixty seconds, one scroll. Not twelve scenes: **one scene**, one
video surface, and scroll position drives a playhead that runs across all twelve
in sequence. There is no scene boundary to fall through, so the whole movement
scrubs as one continuous take — which is what the footage was generated for.
Each clip's last frame was the seed for the next clip's first, so the joins are
graphic matches rather than cuts.

| # | Leg | Clip | In → out | The join into it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | The portal | `j01-portal` | The room's own mark, lit as a ring in the wall, light pouring out of it → inside the ring | It starts in the room we just spent six acts in. The door is on the wall that has been in every act. |
| 02 | The equaliser tunnel | `j02-equalizer` | A tunnel of light bars → the tunnel opens and empties | Ring to ring. The portal's concentric green becomes the tunnel's ribs. |
| 03 | The infinite playlist | `j03-playlist` | A corridor of every playlist ever, a moon at the far end → out over a night city | The tunnel empties into the corridor. Same vanishing point, different walls. |
| 04 | Soundwaves city | `j04-city` | The mark hangs over the city as a moon and pours a green avenue down between the towers → we dive into it | The corridor exits over the city the moon was already sitting in. |
| 05 | The parallax world | `j05-parallax` | The avenue folds into an impossible stair architecture and we fall through it → out through a green ring | Falling forward becomes falling down without the camera ever changing speed. |
| 06 | The vinyl dimension | `j06-vinyl` | A record the size of a city seen from above → the camera drops to the surface and the groove becomes a road | The ring at the end of leg 05 is the record at the start of leg 06. Same circle, new scale. |
| 07 | **The groove highway** | `j07-highway` | A car running the groove between walls of album art → into a ring tunnel | **The drop.** For six legs the world moved past us because we flew. Here something else is moving, and faster than we are. |
| 08 | The listener | `j08-listener` | Through the tunnel into a green earcup, a face, closed eyes → the eye opens | The tunnel mouth and the earcup are the same circle. The whole flight turns out to have been happening about eight centimetres from someone's ear. |
| 09 | Through the pupil | `j09-pupil` | Out of the pupil into deep space where records orbit as planets → down onto one, and its groove is a road again | The iris opens onto space. The road returning is deliberate: it says we are back inside the same record, from the other side. |
| 10 | The pull-back | `j10-pullback` | A figure alone on a black plain under a green aurora → the camera lifts and the sky is a record on a turntable | The reveal the whole movement was for. Everything since the portal was one side of one record playing. |
| 11 | The return | `j11-return` | Floating islands under a green moon, someone sitting → the islands resolve into a room and the moon into a lamp on a shelf | The comedown. The dream folds back into furniture, which is where Side A started. |
| 12 | One line | `j12-line` | An hourglass of green light runs out → everything collapses into a single violet waveform on black | It hands the page back to the film's own Finale — "the groove narrows to a line, the line to a pulse". The last frame of Side B is the first idea of Side A. |

### The stations that did not survive contact with the footage

The journey was designed as ten stations. Two of them have no motion clip in
the batch and the cut does not pretend otherwise:

- **Mood matrix** — in the stills it is a wall of tiles labelled Focus, Chill,
  Energy, Sleep, Party, Romance. In the footage it is the same corridor as the
  infinite playlist, shot the same way. One leg covers both; a second would be
  the same shot twice.
- **The drop** as a still is an explosion of light. Nothing was generated from
  it, so the drop is delivered as a change in physics (leg 07) rather than as a
  picture of an explosion, which is the better version anyway.

### Unused journey takes, and why

- **Galaxy ceiling** — a lounge whose ceiling gives way to real deep space with
  planets and green rays. The strongest single clip I did not ship. It does
  exactly leg 01's job — leave the room — and only one door can open.
- **Floating headphones** — headphones over a lit pedestal, the camera enters
  the earcup and comes out in a corridor of album art. Beaten by leg 08, which
  does the same idea with a face in it.
- **The doorway** — walking in through a door, across the room, to a record on
  the deck. A lovely arrival, but Side A already arrives six times.
- **The equaliser room** — walls of bars around a lounge, ending on headphones
  on a glowing floor ring. Same station as leg 02 with less travel.
- **Violet neon tunnel** — beautiful for four seconds, then Wan cuts inside the
  clip to a moon over a city. A clip with a cut in it cannot sit in a movement
  whose whole claim is that there are no cuts.
- **Dreamscape alternate** — the storm-lit version of leg 11's islands. Leg 11
  wins because it resolves into a room; this one resolves into another city.

## What the scroll hand is doing

This matters more than any single frame, because the hand is the only
instrument the audience is actually playing.

- **Side A acts** — three surfaces per act at ~300 vh each. The hand learns
  that one act costs about three flicks. Rhythm before content.
- **Legs 01–06** — the flight scene is one tall pin, and twelve clips share it,
  so a leg costs a *quarter* of what an act cost. The hand notices it is
  covering ground faster without having changed anything it is doing. That is
  the acceleration the build needs and it is free.
- **Leg 07** — no change in the scroll mapping at all. The speed arrives
  entirely from the picture. Changing the mapping here would feel like the page
  grabbing the wheel.
- **Legs 08–12** — still a quarter each, but the shots are slow, so the same
  hand motion now reads as *lingering*. The comedown is the same gesture over
  slower material, which is exactly what a comedown is.
- **Captions** — one card per leg, arriving on a 12 % cross-fade and sitting at
  a shallower parallax than the plate (the plate travels, the caption drifts).
  Nothing in Side B has a number, a lens, or a frame count on it. Side A is the
  making-of; Side B is the thing being made.

## Two rules held throughout

**The code stays.** Every canvas, SVG and CSS scene that was in the film is
still in the film. Photoreal footage joined the page; it did not evict the
craft. An act that opens on a photograph and closes on a hand-drawn canvas is
making a stronger claim than either could alone.

**The credits tell the truth.** The footer used to say "path-traced, no AI
footage" and "no official artwork reproduced". Both stop being true the moment
this ships, and both are rewritten: keyframes GPT-Image, motion Wan, plates
Blender Cycles, engine cinema.js — and the footage plainly shows the Spotify
logomark, so the disclosure now says unaffiliated fan tribute and names the
trademark holder instead of claiming nothing was reproduced.
